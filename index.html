    Rules

    The cards

    Threefold is played with thirty-six cards made up of three copies of each number from 1 to 12. Most of the numbers carry one of the seven chakras or a sacred image, listed at the end of these rules, though in play the only thing that matters about a card is its number.

    Setting up

    Every player is dealt a hand, and the cards left over are laid face down in the middle of the table. The number of cards in each hand depends on how many people are playing.

    Each hand is kept in order, with the lowest number on the left and the highest on the right. You can see your own cards but nobody else's, and because every hand is sorted the same way, a card shown from the end of someone's hand also tells you a little about the cards beside it.

    Taking a turn

    On your turn you reveal cards one at a time, trying to find three with the same number. Each card can come from one of two places: you can turn over any face-down card in the middle, or you can ask any player, yourself included, for the lowest or the highest card in their hand that has not already been shown this turn. A card taken from a hand is shown to everyone and stays in that hand. On screen, the cards you are allowed to choose rise slightly off the table, and you pick one by clicking or tapping it.

    You keep going for as long as every card you have revealed shares a number. As soon as a card fails to match the ones before it, your turn is over, the revealed cards are turned back face down where they came from, and play moves on to the next player. If you reveal three cards with the same number, you take them as a trio and your turn ends there. There is no way to stop partway through a turn, so it is wise to start with a number you have good reason to think you can find three of.

    Winning

    In Simple mode the first player to collect three trios wins. Spicy mode needs only two trios, although they have to be connected, which means their numbers either add up to 7 or differ by 7. That gives eight connected pairs: 1 and 6, 2 and 5, 3 and 4, 1 and 8, 2 and 9, 3 and 10, 4 and 11, and 5 and 12. In a Spicy game each card shows the numbers it connects with beneath its name. Whichever mode you choose, a trio of 7s wins the game at once.

    Playing on your own

    In single player you face two to five computer players, and the difficulty sets how much they remember and how long revealed cards stay face up. On Normal they forget nearly everything they see and you get the longest look at each reveal, while on Impossible they remember every card that has been turned over and the cards flip back quickly. On the easier levels a computer player holding a pair at either end of its hand will often begin its turn by showing both cards, which tells you where two of that number are.

    Playing with friends

    To play online, choose Multiplayer, enter your name, and either host a game or join one using the five-character room code your host shares with you. Opening the invite link a host sends will fill the code in for you. The host decides how many seats there are, and any seat that no person takes goes to a computer player. Everyone in the room can hear each other while they play, and the Mute button at the top of the screen switches your microphone off and on. When a guest leaves partway through, a computer player takes over their seat so the game can carry on, but when the host leaves, the game ends for everyone.
